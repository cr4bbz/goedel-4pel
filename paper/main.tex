\title{Decomposing Modal Collapse:\\A Four-Valued Analysis of G\"odel--Scott Positivity}
\author{Julian Voigt}
\date{Working manuscript, version 0.3}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
I study the inferential architecture of the G\"odel--Scott ontological theory under a bilateral four-valued semantics that admits both inconsistent and incomplete information. The aim is not to strengthen the ontological conclusion, but to determine whether classically unitary principles, in particular positivity rigidity and modal collapse, decompose into logically distinct positive and negative information channels. I fix Scott's classical theory as a control system, adopt a conservative Belnap--Dunn/FDE propositional kernel, and extend it with a bilateral Kripke semantics that recovers ordinary modal semantics on the classical fragment. I then decompose Scott's positivity axiom A1 into two independent directional information flows and isolate the direct positive-support content of A4. This analysis shows that the previously considered clauses \(\neginfo{P(\varphi)}\Rightarrow\neginfo{\Box P(\varphi)}\) and \(\neginfo{\chi}\Rightarrow\neginfo{\Box\chi}\) are reflexively trivial on the fixed S5 frames and therefore are not informative negative rigidity or collapse principles. Universal negative persistence is instead represented by \(\neginfo{P(\varphi)}\Rightarrow\pos{\Box\neg P(\varphi)}\) and \(\MCm:\neginfo{\chi}\Rightarrow\pos{\Box\neg\chi}\). Strong bilateral A1 together with positive rigidity already entails negative positivity rigidity, revealing A1 as a channel-coupling principle. Finally, local glut and gap counterassignments show that the classical reflection step from God-like exemplification to positivity does not survive the minimal paraconsistent/paracomplete lift without additional consistency and completeness structure. The full collapse classification remains open.
\end{abstract}
