\title{Decomposing Modal Collapse:\\A Four-Valued Analysis of G\"odel--Scott Positivity}
\author{Julian Voigt}
\date{Working manuscript, version 0.2}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
I study the inferential architecture of the G\"odel--Scott ontological theory under a bilateral four-valued semantics that admits both inconsistent and incomplete information. The aim is not to strengthen the ontological conclusion, but to determine whether classically unitary principles, in particular positivity rigidity and modal collapse, decompose into logically distinct positive and negative information channels. I fix Scott's classical theory as a control system and adopt a conservative Belnap--Dunn/FDE propositional kernel with values \(\True=(1,0)\), \(\False=(0,1)\), \(\Both=(1,1)\), and \(\Neither=(0,0)\). I extend this kernel with a bilateral Kripke semantics in which positive necessity is universal over accessible positive support while negative necessity is witnessed by accessible negative support, with the dual pattern for possibility. The modal fragment preserves full bilateral box/diamond duality and recovers ordinary Kripke semantics on the classical fragment. I further exhibit an exact paired-neighborhood representation of the relational semantics, isolating a complement-duality condition under which broader neighborhood models preserve classical modal bivalence. This yields a controlled semantic hierarchy for the later decomposition of positivity rigidity and modal collapse. Higher-order and positivity liftings remain open in this version.
\end{abstract}
