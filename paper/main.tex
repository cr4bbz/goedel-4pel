\title{Decomposing Modal Collapse:\\A Four-Valued Analysis of G\"odel--Scott Positivity}
\author{Julian Voigt}
\date{Working manuscript, version 0.8}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
I study the inferential architecture of the G\"odel--Scott ontological theory under a bilateral four-valued semantics admitting both inconsistent and incomplete information. Scott's classical theory is fixed as a control system and reconstructed incrementally over a Belnap--Dunn/FDE propositional kernel, bilateral Kripke modality, actualist individual quantification, and higher-order property quantification. The original conjecture that positive and negative modal collapse might separate is falsified under involutive FDE negation: the informative signed collapse schemata are equivalent by substitution. The substantive decomposition instead occurs upstream. Scott's A1 splits into two independent directional information flows with distinct proof-theoretic roles, and mere positive support is insufficient for the T1 analogue because positivity gluts absorb the classical reductio. I define support-based bilateral Godlikeness, essence, and necessary existence without fixing a four-valued object-language conditional. Two explicit two-world S5 models show that the natural essence semantics does not make Godlikeness an essence automatically: positive T2 fails separately through glut and gap mechanisms. A relevant consistency/completeness package is sufficient to recover T2, and Lean 4.30.0 machine-checks this implication. Gate 8 sharpens and compares the resulting architecture. Once Scott T2 is available, symmetry alone suffices for the positive T3 step; a reflexive-transitive but non-symmetric finite model refutes the corresponding S4 replacement. A project-internal exact positive Godlikeness condition internalizes local reflection and reduces its T2 route to exactness plus positive rigidity while remaining compatible with gluts. A literature-grounded Anderson interface, extended by project-specific bilateral negative evidence, admits a two-world model with positive necessary Godlike existence but failure of positive modal collapse, and its T3 branch likewise reduces to symmetry. Finally, a Fitting comparison is begun by enforcing the intension/extension distinction at the Lean type level, with positivity typed over property extensions rather than intensions. The finite oracle also retains the earlier bounded result: 873 two-world one-entity models satisfy the full Scott T2 recovery package and all satisfy T2, while dropping any one of its four current recovery assumptions yields a countermodel in that family. The remaining questions are explicitly global: whether the Scott T2 package can be weakened beyond these bounded results, and which structural features of Godlikeness and essence govern collapse across the comparative variants.
\end{abstract}
