\title{Decomposing Modal Collapse:\\A Four-Valued Analysis of G\"odel--Scott Positivity}
\author{Julian Voigt}
\date{Working manuscript, version 0.4}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
I study the inferential architecture of the G\"odel--Scott ontological theory under a bilateral four-valued semantics admitting both inconsistent and incomplete information. I fix Scott's classical theory as a control system, adopt a conservative Belnap--Dunn/FDE propositional kernel, and extend it with a bilateral Kripke semantics recovering ordinary modal semantics on the classical fragment. Scott's positivity axiom A1 decomposes into two independent directional information flows, while the positive and universal-negative persistence components of positivity rigidity are independent in the absence of A1 but become interderivable under strong bilateral A1. A central negative result is that the informative positive and negative modal-collapse schemata do not themselves separate: because FDE negation is involutive and the schemata range over all formulas, positive collapse and universal negative persistence are equivalent by substitution. The non-classical decomposition therefore occurs upstream. In particular, the classical reflection step from God-like exemplification to positivity fails both by glut and by gap mechanisms. At an abstract proof interface I identify a locally minimal recovery package consisting of one direction of A1, a positive Godlikeness support condition, consistency of relevant exemplification, and completeness of relevant positivity information. Adding positive rigidity, global God-like witness supply, and constant-property embedding yields a conditional interface-level derivation of modal collapse. The remaining task is to determine which of these interfaces are justified, weakened, or replaced by the full four-valued reconstruction of A2, A3, Godlikeness, and quantification.
\end{abstract}
