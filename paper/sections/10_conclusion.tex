\section{Conclusion and Research Agenda}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This manuscript begins from a deliberately narrow claim. The G\"odel--Scott theory is not merely an object to be translated from two values to four. It is a controlled test case for asking whether classically unitary modal principles conceal distinct channels of positive and negative information.

At version 0.1 three pieces are in place. First, the Scott theory is frozen as a classical control system. Second, the propositional semantics is a conservative Belnap--Dunn/FDE kernel that is paraconsistent, paracomplete, and classically recoverable. Third, the classical modal-collapse proof has been reduced to a dependency spine that identifies A1, A4, D1, T3, and frame conditions as the principal interfaces to inspect.

The next step is semantic rather than theological: Gate 2 must determine the modal lift. Only then is it meaningful to test whether
\[
  \MCp\not\equiv\MCm
\]
or whether the remaining G\"odel--Scott structure couples the two channels back together. Either outcome would answer a substantive structural question.

The paper will be revised gate by gate. Open sections are intentionally part of the artifact: they make explicit which claims the current theory has not yet earned.
