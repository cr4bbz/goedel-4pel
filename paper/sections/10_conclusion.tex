\section{Conclusion and Research Agenda}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This manuscript begins from a deliberately narrow claim. The G\"odel--Scott theory is not merely an object to be translated from two values to four. It is a controlled test case for asking whether classically unitary modal principles conceal distinct channels of positive and negative information.

At version 0.2 four pieces are in place. First, the Scott theory is frozen as a classical control system. Second, the propositional semantics is a conservative Belnap--Dunn/FDE kernel that is paraconsistent, paracomplete, and classically recoverable. Third, the classical modal-collapse proof has been reduced to a dependency spine that identifies A1, A4, D1, T3, and frame conditions as the principal interfaces to inspect. Fourth, the modal layer is fixed by a bilateral relational semantics with full box/diamond duality and classical recovery, together with an exact paired-neighborhood representation that exposes a controlled route beyond Kripke frames.

The next step is the first genuinely G\"odel-specific non-classical experiment: Gate 3 must determine how A1 and A4 split across positive and negative information channels while the S5 modal control semantics remains fixed. Only then is it meaningful to test whether
\[
  \MCp\not\equiv\MCm
\]
or whether the remaining G\"odel--Scott structure couples the two channels back together. Either outcome would answer a substantive structural question.

The paper will be revised gate by gate. Open sections are intentionally part of the artifact: they make explicit which claims the current theory has not yet earned.
