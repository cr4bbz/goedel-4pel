\section{Reconstructing the G\"odel--Scott Chain}
\label{sec:reconstruction}

Gates 5 and 6 reconstruct the higher-order existence branch through A2, A3, Godlikeness, essence, necessary existence, A5, and a positive analogue of Scott's T3. The reconstruction keeps the object-language four-valued conditional unfixed; implication-sensitive clauses are represented semantically.

\subsection{Actualist quantification and necessary positive entailment}

I retain Scott's actualist quantification over individuals using a classical metalanguage existence relation \(E(w,x)\). Positive and negative existential satisfaction are
\[
  w\models^+\exists^E x\,\varphi(x)
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \exists x\,(E(w,x)\land w\models^+\varphi(x)),
\]
\[
  w\models^-\exists^E x\,\varphi(x)
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \forall x\,(E(w,x)\Rightarrow w\models^-\varphi(x)),
\]
with the dual universal clauses.

Necessary positive entailment is the semantic relation
\[
  \operatorname{NEnt}^{+}_{E}(\varphi,\psi,w),
\]
which holds iff at every world accessible from \(w\), every actually existing positive instance of \(\varphi\) is a positive instance of \(\psi\). The control lifting of A2 is
\[
  A2^+:\quad
  \pos{P(\varphi)}\land
  \operatorname{NEnt}^{+}_{E}(\varphi,\psi)
  \Rightarrow
  \pos{P(\psi)}.
\]

For Gate 6 I also use negative evidence against necessary entailment:
\[
  \operatorname{NEnt}^{-}_{E}(\varphi,\psi,w)
\]
iff some accessible actually existing object positively exemplifies \(\varphi\) and negatively exemplifies \(\psi\). On the classical fragment these two relations reproduce truth and falsity of the necessary-entailment condition.

\subsection{Truth-only positivity and possible exemplification}

Define
\[
  \operatorname{Pos}_{T}(\varphi)
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \pos{P(\varphi)}
  \text{ and not }\neginfo{P(\varphi)},
\]
where ``not'' is metalanguage denial.

\begin{theorem}[Truth-only positive properties are possibly exemplified]
Assume globally \(A1_R\) and \(A2^+\). Then
\[
  \operatorname{Pos}_{T}(\varphi)
  \Rightarrow
  \pos{\Diamond\exists^E x\,\varphi(x)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof is the Gate-5 reductio: failure of positive possible exemplification makes \(\operatorname{NEnt}^{+}_{E}(\varphi,\neg\varphi)\) vacuously true; A2 then gives \(\pos{P(\neg\varphi)}\), and \(A1_R\) yields \(\neginfo{P(\varphi)}\), contradicting truth-only positivity. A one-world model with an empty actual domain and glutty positivity shows that the stronger antecedent \(\pos{P(\varphi)}\) is insufficient.

Scott A3 is therefore split into
\[
  A3^+:\quad \pos{P(G)},
  \qquad
  A3_T:\quad \operatorname{Pos}_{T}(G).
\]
The control branch uses \(A3_T\), obtaining
\[
  \boxed{\pos{\Diamond\exists^E x\,G(x)}}.
\]

\subsection{Support-based Godlikeness}

The control definition \(G\text{-sup-v0.1}\) is
\[
  w\models^+G(x)
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \forall\varphi\,
  \bigl(w\models^+P(\varphi)\Rightarrow w\models^+\varphi(x)\bigr),
\]
\[
  w\models^-G(x)
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \exists\varphi\,
  \bigl(w\models^+P(\varphi)\land w\models^-\varphi(x)\bigr).
\]
This recovers Scott D1 on the classical fragment, discharges the former \(D1^+\) interface, and permits all four information states for Godlikeness. Full higher-order lambda abstraction supplies the constant-property interface \(CONST\).

\subsection{Bilateral essence}

The Gate-6 control definition \(Ess\text{-sup-v0.1}\) is
\[
\begin{split}
  w\models^+(\varphi\operatorname{Ess}x)
  \Longleftrightarrow{}&
  w\models^+\varphi(x)\\
  &\land
  \forall\psi\,
  \bigl(w\models^+\psi(x)
  \Rightarrow
  \operatorname{NEnt}^{+}_{E}(\varphi,\psi,w)\bigr),
\end{split}
\]
while
\[
\begin{split}
  w\models^-(\varphi\operatorname{Ess}x)
  \Longleftrightarrow{}&
  w\models^-\varphi(x)\\
  &\lor
  \exists\psi\,
  \bigl(w\models^+\psi(x)
  \land
  \operatorname{NEnt}^{-}_{E}(\varphi,\psi,w)\bigr).
\end{split}
\]
On the classical fragment these clauses recover Scott D2.

The positive T2 target is
\[
  T2^+:\quad
  \pos{G(x)}\Rightarrow\pos{G\operatorname{Ess}x}.
\]

\paragraph{T2 is not automatic.}
Two explicit two-world S5 models refute \(T2^+\) while satisfying the Gate-5 control stack together with strong A1 and \(R^+\). In the glut model, a property \(Z\) is both positively and negatively exemplified by one positive God-like witness but lacks positive support at another accessible God-like witness. In the gap model, \(P(Z)=P(\neg Z)=\Neither\), while \(Z\) is positively exemplified locally but not necessarily shared. In both cases \(A2^+\), \(A3_T\), \(G\text{-sup-v0.1}\), strong A1, and positive rigidity hold, yet \(\operatorname{NEnt}^{+}_{E}(G,Z)\) fails. Hence positive Godlikeness does not imply positive Godlikeness-as-essence merely by choosing a natural bilateral D2.

\subsection{A sufficient recovery route for T2}

For properties actually positively exemplified by positive God-like witnesses, define
\[
  COMP_P^G:
  \quad
  \pos{G(x)}\land\pos{\psi(x)}
  \Rightarrow
  \bigl(\pos{P(\psi)}\text{ or }\neginfo{P(\psi)}\bigr),
\]
and
\[
  CONS_G^G:
  \quad
  \pos{G(x)}\land\pos{\psi(x)}
  \Rightarrow
  \text{not }\neginfo{\psi(x)}.
\]
Let \(REG_G\) be their conjunction.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional recovery of positive T2]
Under \(A1_L\), \(R^+\), \(G\text{-sup-v0.1}\), and \(REG_G\),
\[
  T2^+
\]
holds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(\pos{G(x)}\) and let \(\psi\) be positively exemplified by \(x\). Relevant positivity completeness gives \(\pos{P(\psi)}\) or \(\neginfo{P(\psi)}\). The latter alternative would, by \(A1_L\), make \(\neg\psi\) positively positive; Godlikeness would then yield \(\neginfo{\psi(x)}\), contradicting relevant exemplification consistency. Hence \(\pos{P(\psi)}\). Positive rigidity makes this support necessary, and every accessible positive God-like witness consequently positively exemplifies \(\psi\). Thus \(\operatorname{NEnt}^{+}_{E}(G,\psi)\), as required by positive essence.
\end{proof}

The glut and gap countermodels isolate the two regularity failures for this recovery route. I do not claim that \(REG_G\) is globally necessary for every model satisfying \(T2^+\).

\subsection{Bilateral necessary existence and A5}

Define \(NE\text{-sup-v0.1}\) by
\[
  w\models^+NE(x)
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \forall\varphi\,
  \bigl(
  w\models^+(\varphi\operatorname{Ess}x)
  \Rightarrow
  w\models^+\Box\exists^E y\,\varphi(y)
  \bigr),
\]
\[
  w\models^-NE(x)
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \exists\varphi\,
  \bigl(
  w\models^+(\varphi\operatorname{Ess}x)
  \land
  w\models^-\Box\exists^E y\,\varphi(y)
  \bigr).
\]
These clauses recover Scott D3 classically.

For A5 distinguish
\[
  A5^+:\quad \pos{P(NE)},
  \qquad
  A5_T:\quad \operatorname{Pos}_{T}(NE).
\]
Unlike A3, the positive T3 branch needs only \(A5^+\). Once \(\pos{G(x)}\) is available, \(G\text{-sup-v0.1}\) turns \(\pos{P(NE)}\) directly into \(\pos{NE(x)}\). Truth-only positivity of \(NE\) is unnecessary for that positive inference.

\subsection{Positive necessary God-like existence}

\begin{theorem}[Positive T3]
Assume positive possible God-like existence, \(T2^+\), \(A5^+\), \(G\text{-sup-v0.1}\), \(NE\text{-sup-v0.1}\), and the fixed S5 frame conditions. Then
\[
  T3^+:\quad
  \pos{\Box\exists^E x\,G(x)}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At an accessible world containing a positive God-like witness \(x\), A5 and Godlikeness yield \(\pos{NE(x)}\), while T2 gives \(\pos{G\operatorname{Ess}x}\). Positive necessary existence therefore yields \(\pos{\Box\exists^E y\,G(y)}\) at that world. Hence the original world satisfies \(\pos{\Diamond\Box\exists^E y\,G(y)}\). On S5 frames positive support validates \(\Diamond\Box\theta\Rightarrow\Box\theta\), so \(T3^+\) follows.
\end{proof}

Because the S5 control frame is reflexive, \(T3^+\) immediately supplies an actually existing positive God-like witness at every world. Thus the former interface \(GW\) is discharged.

Combining the sufficient T2 route with Gate 5 gives the conditional positive Scott-control chain
\[
  A1_{\leftrightarrow}
  +A2^+
  +A3_T
  +R^+
  +REG_G
  +A5^+
  \Longrightarrow
  T3^+,
\]
under the fixed higher-order semantics.

\subsection{Essence-compressed modal collapse}

Once \(T2^+\) and \(T3^+\) are available, the earlier reflection/rigidity proof can be compressed.

\begin{theorem}[Essence-compressed collapse]
Under \(T2^+\), \(T3^+\), and constant-property embedding,
\[
  \MCp
\]
holds, and therefore \(\MCm\) holds by the Gate-4 collapse-equivalence theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(\pos{\chi}\) at \(w\). T3 supplies an actual positive God-like witness \(x\) at \(w\), and T2 makes \(G\) a positive essence of \(x\). For the constant property \(Z_\chi=\lambda y.\chi\), \(\pos{Z_\chi(x)}\). Positive essence gives \(\operatorname{NEnt}^{+}_{E}(G,Z_\chi,w)\). T3 supplies a positive God-like witness at every accessible world, so each such witness positively exemplifies \(Z_\chi\), hence each accessible world positively supports \(\chi\). Therefore \(\pos{\Box\chi}\).
\end{proof}

This result refines the Gate-4 dependency map. The earlier \(REF^+\), \(CONS_G\), \(COMP_P\), and \(R^+\) assumptions are ingredients of one route for deriving T2 from lower-level principles; they are not separate premises of the essence-compressed collapse theorem after T2 and T3 have already been established.

\subsection{Robustness and the new bottleneck}

A5 illustrates genuine inconsistency tolerance. If \(P(NE)=\Both\), strong A1 also permits \(P(\neg NE)=\Both\). A positive God-like witness may therefore satisfy both \(\pos{NE(x)}\) and \(\neginfo{NE(x)}\), while the positive component remains sufficient for the T3 derivation.

Likewise, global positivity completeness and global consistency of arbitrary God-like exemplification are not semantic prerequisites for every model satisfying the positive T2/T3 chain. They are route-specific regularity assumptions used to recover T2 from the primitive lower-level axioms.

The decisive open question after Gate 6 is consequently not whether necessary existence can supply witnesses. It can, once T2 is available. The main remaining semantic-minimality problem is:
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{What are the weakest principled four-valued conditions under which }T2^+\text{ follows?}
  }
\]
