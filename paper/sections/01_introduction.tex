\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

G\"odel's ontological argument and Dana Scott's well-known modification form an unusually compact laboratory for higher-order modal reasoning. Their interest for the present paper is structural rather than theological. The theory combines a primitive predicate of positive properties, modal rigidity, higher-order quantification, essence, and necessary existence, while its derivational behavior has been studied extensively with automated and interactive theorem provers \cite{BenzmuellerScott2025,BenzmuellerFuenmayor2019}. In Scott's variant the main existence theorem is accompanied by the well-known phenomenon of \emph{modal collapse}, schematically
\[
  \chi \rightarrow \Box\chi,
\]
according to which every truth is necessary.

Recent machine-checked work sharpens the diagnosis of this phenomenon. In particular, the existence of an ultrafilter-like structure among positive properties does not by itself force collapse; rigidity of positivity is a principal modal ingredient \cite{Benzmueller2026Collapse}. This raises a natural question that classical two-valued semantics cannot express: is the relevant rigidity itself informationally unitary?

The present project studies this question in a bilateral four-valued setting. Instead of assigning a single Boolean value to a formula, I track positive and negative support independently. A formula may therefore be supported only, opposed only, both supported and opposed, or neither. This gives the four values
\[
  \True=(1,0),\qquad
  \False=(0,1),\qquad
  \Both=(1,1),\qquad
  \Neither=(0,0),
\]
following the Belnap--Dunn/FDE tradition \cite{Belnap1977,Dunn1976}.

The central research question is:

\begin{question}[Bilateral rigidity and collapse]
Under a four-valued bilateral modal semantics, which positive and negative components of the G\"odel--Scott positivity principles are necessary or sufficient for corresponding positive and negative components of modal collapse?
\end{question}

The first candidate decomposition is
\[
  \MCp:\quad \pos{\chi}\Rightarrow\pos{\Box\chi},
  \qquad
  \MCm:\quad \neginfo{\chi}\Rightarrow\neginfo{\Box\chi},
\]
where \(\Rightarrow\) is, at this stage, a metalanguage condition rather than an object-language conditional. Likewise, Scott's rigidity axiom
\[
  P(\varphi)\rightarrow\Box P(\varphi)
\]
may admit distinct bilateral components \(\Rp\) and \(\Rm\).

The contribution sought is therefore not a ``stronger proof of God''. The intended contribution is a structural decomposition theorem, separation result, robustness result, or explanatory countermodel showing exactly how inconsistency and incompleteness alter the inferential architecture of the Scott theory.

\paragraph{Methodological discipline.}
The development proceeds conservatively. I first freeze a classical Scott baseline, then fix only the propositional four-valued kernel. Modal semantics, the lifting of positivity, higher-order quantification, and any four-valued conditional are treated as separate design decisions. This prevents a syntactically convenient translation from silently deciding the main research question.
