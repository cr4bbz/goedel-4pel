\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

G\"odel's ontological argument and Dana Scott's well-known modification form an unusually compact laboratory for higher-order modal reasoning. Their interest for the present paper is structural rather than theological. The theory combines a primitive predicate of positive properties, modal rigidity, higher-order quantification, essence, and necessary existence, while its derivational behavior has been studied extensively with automated and interactive theorem provers \cite{BenzmuellerScott2025,BenzmuellerFuenmayor2019}. In Scott's variant the main existence theorem is accompanied by the well-known phenomenon of \emph{modal collapse}, schematically
\[
  \chi \rightarrow \Box\chi,
\]
according to which every truth is necessary.

Recent machine-checked work sharpens the diagnosis of this phenomenon. In particular, the existence of an ultrafilter-like structure among positive properties does not by itself force collapse; rigidity of positivity is a principal modal ingredient \cite{Benzmueller2026Collapse}. This raises a natural question that classical two-valued semantics cannot express: which information flows inside positivity and rigidity are actually responsible for the collapse mechanism?

I study this question in a bilateral four-valued setting. Instead of assigning a single Boolean value to a formula, I track positive and negative support independently. A formula may therefore be supported only, opposed only, both supported and opposed, or neither. This gives the four values
\[
  \True=(1,0),\qquad
  \False=(0,1),\qquad
  \Both=(1,1),\qquad
  \Neither=(0,0),
\]
following the Belnap--Dunn/FDE tradition \cite{Belnap1977,Dunn1976}.

The central research question is:

\begin{question}[Upstream structure of modal collapse]
Under a four-valued bilateral modal semantics, which components of positivity-negation transfer, positivity rigidity, informational regularity, and higher-order witness structure reconstruct the classical G\"odel--Scott modal-collapse mechanism?
\end{question}

The informative signed collapse pair is
\[
  \MCp:\quad \pos{\chi}\Rightarrow\pos{\Box\chi},
  \qquad
  \MCm:\quad \neginfo{\chi}\Rightarrow\pos{\Box\neg\chi}.
\]
Gate 4 shows that these are not independent in the fixed FDE setting. Since \(\neginfo{\chi}\) is equivalent to \(\pos{\neg\chi}\), \(\MCm\) is exactly \(\MCp\) instantiated at \(\neg\chi\), and conversely by involutive negation. The original collapse-separation conjecture is therefore falsified rather than preserved by an ad hoc semantic modification.

The decomposition instead occurs upstream. Scott's A1 splits into two independent directional information flows,
\[
  A1_L:\quad \neginfo{P(\varphi)}\Rightarrow\pos{P(\neg\varphi)},
\]
\[
  A1_R:\quad \pos{P(\neg\varphi)}\Rightarrow\neginfo{P(\varphi)},
\]
while positive and universal-negative positivity rigidity are independent without A1 but become interderivable under strong A1. The local classical reflection step from God-like exemplification to positivity additionally fails through distinct glut and gap mechanisms.

The contribution sought is therefore not a ``stronger proof of God''. The intended contribution is an explanatory dependency analysis showing where four-valued inconsistency and incompleteness alter the inferential architecture of the Scott theory, including negative results that identify seemingly natural decompositions as impossible under the chosen semantics.

\paragraph{Methodological discipline.}
The development proceeds conservatively. I freeze a classical Scott baseline, fix a standard four-valued propositional kernel, extend it with a bilateral Kripke modal control semantics, and only then lift individual G\"odel--Scott principles. Failed conjectures are retained as results rather than repaired by changing the semantics after the fact. Higher-order quantification and any four-valued object-language conditional remain separate design decisions until the reconstruction requires them.

\paragraph{Status of this manuscript.}
Version 0.4 reports the classical baseline, propositional and modal semantics, the directional decomposition of A1, the rigidity-coupling result, the collapse-channel equivalence theorem, the local minimality analysis of the reflection bridge, and a conditional interface-level collapse theorem. The next stage is the full four-valued reconstruction of A2, A3, Godlikeness, and possible exemplification.
