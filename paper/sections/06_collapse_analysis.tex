\section{From Positivity Rigidity to Modal Collapse}
\label{sec:collapse}

The classical proof structure identifies a narrow pressure point for the four-valued analysis. Fix a world \(w\), an individual \(x\), and a property \(Z\). In the Scott theory,
\[
  G(x)@w,\; Z(x)@w
\]
forces \(P(Z)@w\): if \(Z\) were non-positive, A1 would make \(\neg Z\) positive, while D1 would then force the God-like \(x\) to exemplify \(\neg Z\), contradicting \(Z(x)\). A4 then yields \(\Box P(Z)\), and D1 propagates \(Z\) to God-like entities at accessible worlds. Thus the local classical spine is
\[
  G(x),Z(x)
  \xRightarrow{A1+D1} P(Z)
  \xRightarrow{A4} \Box P(Z)
  \xRightarrow{D1} \Box\forall z\,(G(z)\rightarrow Z(z)).
\]

Substituting the constant-in-individuals property \(Z:=\lambda z.\chi\) embeds an arbitrary proposition \(\chi\) into this mechanism. Together with T3, which supplies necessarily existing God-like witnesses, and suitable frame conditions, the standard Scott setting yields
\[
  \chi\rightarrow\Box\chi.
\]
The detailed classical dependency analysis is maintained in \texttt{docs/MODAL\_COLLAPSE\_SPINE.md}. This local structure is consonant with the recent machine-checked diagnosis that positivity rigidity, rather than ultrafilter structure alone, is a principal driver of collapse \cite{Benzmueller2026Collapse}.

\subsection{Correct bilateral collapse targets}

The informative signed persistence principles are
\[
  \MCp:\quad \pos{\chi}\Rightarrow\pos{\Box\chi}
\]
and
\[
  \MCm:\quad \neginfo{\chi}\Rightarrow\pos{\Box\neg\chi}.
\]
The earlier candidate \(\neginfo{\chi}\Rightarrow\neginfo{\Box\chi}\) is discarded because it follows automatically on reflexive frames: the current world itself witnesses negative satisfaction of \(\Box\chi\).

\begin{theorem}[Collapse-channel equivalence]
Under the fixed FDE negation, with both schemata ranging over all formulas,
\[
  \MCp\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\MCm.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(\MCp\) and \(\neginfo{\chi}\). By FDE negation, \(\pos{\neg\chi}\). Instantiating \(\MCp\) with \(\neg\chi\) gives \(\pos{\Box\neg\chi}\), hence \(\MCm\).

Conversely, assume \(\MCm\) and \(\pos{\chi}\). Then \(\neginfo{\neg\chi}\). Instantiating \(\MCm\) with \(\neg\chi\) yields \(\pos{\Box\neg\neg\chi}\), which is equivalent to \(\pos{\Box\chi}\) because FDE negation is involutive.
\end{proof}

Thus the original conjecture that the informative positive and negative collapse channels might separate is falsified for the present semantics. The decomposition must be sought upstream rather than forced at the collapse schema itself.

\subsection{Rigidity separates before A1 couples it}

Write
\[
  \Rp:\quad \pos{P(\varphi)}\Rightarrow\pos{\Box P(\varphi)}
\]
and
\[
  \Rm:\quad \neginfo{P(\varphi)}\Rightarrow\pos{\Box\neg P(\varphi)}.
\]
Without A1 these principles are independent. On the two-world S5 frame with universal accessibility, assign a property \(\psi\) the positivity values
\[
  v_{w_0}(P(\psi))=\Neither,
  \qquad
  v_{w_1}(P(\psi))=\False.
\]
If all other relevant positivity statements receive \(\Neither\), then \(\Rp\) holds vacuously while \(\Rm\) fails at \(w_1\). Replacing \(\False\) by \(\True\) yields the converse separation: \(\Rm\) holds vacuously while \(\Rp\) fails.

Strong A1 destroys this independence.

\begin{proposition}[Rigidity coupling under strong A1]
Under \(A1_L+A1_R\) and involutive property negation,
\[
  \Rp\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Rm.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The direction \(\Rp\Rightarrow\Rm\) was established in the positivity analysis. For the converse, suppose \(\pos{P(\varphi)}\). By \(A1_R\), instantiated at \(\neg\varphi\), one obtains \(\neginfo{P(\neg\varphi)}\). Applying \(\Rm\) to \(\neg\varphi\) yields \(\pos{\Box\neg P(\neg\varphi)}\). At each accessible world, \(A1_L\) converts \(\neginfo{P(\neg\varphi)}\) back into \(\pos{P(\varphi)}\). Hence \(\pos{\Box P(\varphi)}\).
\end{proof}

The genuine bilateral separation therefore occurs at the level of positivity rigidity only in the absence of the full A1 coupling.

\subsection{Minimal recovery of the local reflection bridge}

The signed reflection target is
\[
  REF^+:\quad \pos{G(x)}\land\pos{Z(x)}\Rightarrow\pos{P(Z)}.
\]
Strong A1 plus the minimal support interface
\[
  D1^+:\quad
  \pos{G(x)}\land\pos{P(\varphi)}\Rightarrow\pos{\varphi(x)}
\]
does not suffice: the glut and gap assignments analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:positivity} block the classical reductio in different ways.

Introduce two explicit metalanguage regularity conditions for the relevant witness and property:
\[
  CONS_G:\quad
  \pos{G(x)}\land\pos{Z(x)}
  \Rightarrow
  \text{not }\neginfo{Z(x)},
\]
and
\[
  COMP_P:\quad
  \pos{P(Z)}\ \text{or}\ \neginfo{P(Z)}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Local reflection recovery]
At the abstract Gate-4 interface,
\[
  A1_L+D1^++CONS_G+COMP_P\models REF^+.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(\pos{G(x)}\) and \(\pos{Z(x)}\). By \(COMP_P\), either \(\pos{P(Z)}\) or \(\neginfo{P(Z)}\). In the latter case, \(A1_L\) yields \(\pos{P(\neg Z)}\), and \(D1^+\) then gives \(\pos{\neg Z(x)}\), equivalently \(\neginfo{Z(x)}\). This contradicts \(CONS_G\). Hence the negative-positivity alternative is excluded and \(\pos{P(Z)}\) remains.
\end{proof}

The package is locally minimal relative to this interface vocabulary. Removing \(A1_L\), \(D1^+\), \(CONS_G\), or \(COMP_P\) respectively admits a counterassignment while the other three remain satisfiable. The glut counterassignment witnesses the need for \(CONS_G\); the gap counterassignment witnesses the need for \(COMP_P\).

\subsection{Conditional collapse kernel}

Two higher-order interfaces are kept explicit because the final quantifier and Godlikeness semantics have not yet been frozen:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \(GW\): every world has some positively supported God-like witness;
  \item \(CONST\): every modal formula \(\chi\) is representable by the constant intensional property \(Z_\chi:=\lambda x.\chi\), preserving both signed satisfaction conditions.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Interface-level collapse package]
Assume globally
\[
  A1_L+D1^++CONS_G+COMP_P+\Rp+GW+CONST.
\]
Then \(\MCp\), and therefore also \(\MCm\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(\pos{\chi}\) at \(w\). By \(GW\), choose \(x\) with \(\pos{G(x)}\) at \(w\). By \(CONST\), \(\pos{Z_\chi(x)}\). The local reflection proposition yields \(\pos{P(Z_\chi)}\). By \(\Rp\), \(\pos{\Box P(Z_\chi)}\).

Let \(v\) be accessible from \(w\). Then \(\pos{P(Z_\chi)}\) at \(v\). By \(GW\), choose \(y\) with \(\pos{G(y)}\) at \(v\). By \(D1^+\), \(\pos{Z_\chi(y)}\), and by \(CONST\), \(\pos{\chi}\) at \(v\). Since \(v\) was arbitrary, \(\pos{\Box\chi}\) at \(w\).
\end{proof}

This is deliberately an interface-level theorem rather than a theorem of the complete four-valued Gödel--Scott theory. Gate 5 must determine whether the final treatment of A2, A3, D1, and higher-order quantification justifies \(GW\) and \(CONST\), and whether the regularity assumptions can be weakened or derived rather than postulated.

\paragraph{Gate-4 conclusion.}
The strongest current structural claim is therefore not that modal collapse itself splits. Under symmetric FDE negation it does not. The non-classical decomposition occurs upstream: positivity rigidity separates before strong A1 recouples it, while the classical reflection bridge additionally relies on consistency and completeness conditions that gluts and gaps defeat independently.
